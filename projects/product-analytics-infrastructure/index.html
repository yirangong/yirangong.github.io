BACKGROUND

THE PROBLEM

All 40+ video templates appeared as one aggregated line. Worse, generations rescued by fallback were still counted as failures, so teams could not tell what to promote, fix, or retire.

WHAT I DID

Rebuilt measurement around one row per generation, defined failure by the user’s final outcome, added seven outcome modes, and tied each failure to its template, model, vendor, and step.

IMPACT

Outcome-based analytics that Content Ops monitors daily and product reviews weekly.

B · ONE REQUEST, COUNTED TWO WAYS

The old dashboard measured deployment paths rather than complete generation requests. A primary failure and the fallback that rescued it appeared as separate series, so the system could describe an endpoint accurately while misrepresenting what happened to the person waiting for a video.

REFLECTION

A metric definition is a product decision

WHAT I LEARNED

There was no external ground truth for whether a fallback-rescued generation “really” failed. Trust came from an inspectable definition, and a stable unit of analysis mattered more than a stable implementation.

WHAT’S STILL OPEN

Success rate does not capture whether an output was worth keeping. The implementation still needs manual validation, and fallback semantics remain less detailed on migrated workflows.
